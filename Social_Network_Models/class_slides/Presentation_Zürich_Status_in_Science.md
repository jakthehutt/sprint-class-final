►Sociology of Science highlights the importance of social processes for scientific
collaboration and knowledge production (Kuhn 1962; Merton 1973; Bourdieu
1988; Latour 1987, Crane 1972)
►Scientific work is marked by inequality in social recognition (Newman 2001; Eom
& Fortunato 2011), segregation by organizational status (Ma et al. 2020), and 
unequal access to resources such as funding (Bol et al. 2018)
►We investigate the scientific field of neuroblastoma research to study the 
development of stratification and segregation in scientific collaboration networks
Background
1


--- PAGE BREAK ---

►Previous studies report inequality in collaboration partners (Newman 2001) 
and illustrate that the number and prestige of collaboration partners are 
crucial for individuals’ careers (Li et al. 2019). Yet, empirical accounts seldom 
investigate how inequality in science evolves in the first place and changes 
over time 
►Application of network models to scientific networks still rare (cf., Kronegger
et al. 2021), convergence issues, lacking comparability of estimates, 
mechanical link between size and network measures (Anderson et al. 1999)
►We overcome these issues by using recent advances in network analysis 
(Duxbury 2021) and studied neuroblastoma research from its genesis onward
Research Gap
2


--- PAGE BREAK ---

►Researchers channel attention on a small set of others: you cannot read, meet or 
collaborate with everyone, selection is unavoidable (see Fiske 2011, Dunbar 2008; Podolny 
2010) 
►Even if a author is only slightly better known than others this will greatly enhance her 
chance to attract more and more attention (Matthew effect, Merton 1968)
►New fields lack resources and legitimization, cycles of accumulation hard to initiate (Latour 
and Woolgar 1986)
H1: Inequality in the distribution of coauthorship ties increases as a scientific field matures and 
grows. 
Theory – Inequality

--- PAGE BREAK ---

Theory – Accumulation dynamics and homogeneity
4
►Previous accounts described the temporal unfolding of research communities and 
observed that actors can draw upon multiple field-specific resources to mobilize 
additional collaborators as fields mature (Chubin, 1976; Frickel and Gross, 2005; Griffith 
and Mullins, 1972). 
►Status theories suggest that status signals diversify as uncertainty linked to field size 
increases (Mayhew 1976, Podolny 2010)
H2: Accumulation dynamics diversify as a scientific field matures and grows. 
►Elites develop due to the accumulation of resources and recognition, thereby the status 
of collaborators becomes more important and increasingly overpowers the tendency for 
local, status similar collaboration (Blau 1994; Burris 2004; Ma et al. 2020)
H3: Scientists who are dissimilar in their years of experience, productivity, and seniority 
collaborate at the outset of a field. This tendency weakens in the later stages of a field’s 
development, and collaboration increasingly exhibits status homogeneity.


--- PAGE BREAK ---

►Neuroblastoma—most frequent solid tumor in childhood —global research collaborations 
recorded at Advances in Neuroblastoma Research conferences (ANR); (Berthold et al. 
2019)
►Record of all abstracts handed in to 18 conferences in a time span of 41 years (1975-2016)
Advantages of the data:
1. Emergence of interdisciplinary research field organized around a clear-cut research topic 
2. Conference co-authorships as indicator of rather substantial collaboration 
3. Conferences as events are important for the evolution of a scientific field 
Empirical setting
6
5


--- PAGE BREAK ---

Data
8459 authors affiliated to 553 institutions of 53 countries presented 3,993 abstracts. 
The number of coauthors increased over the years from 2 to 7 (Berthold et al. 2019). 
Measures
Networks: collaboration networks derived from abstracted handed in to the ANR 
conference series. We included only actors who appeared on at least two abstracts 
at two different conferences (ca. 40% of the initial sample) 
Experience: years since entering the field 
Productivity: publication record in neuroblastoma research (Web of science 
database)
Seniority: share of last author publications

accumulation dynamics

►Inequality in social recognition increased with field size and maturity above 
and beyond the mechanical link between size and graph level indices  
►Multiple status markers matter for researcher’s popularity as the field evolves
►Segregation according to status differences increases over time
►Methodological contribution: application of AME to large networks of 
scientific collaboration for the first time, comparison of effect sizes across 
network models  
Conclusion
20


--- PAGE BREAK ---

►Lack of information on classical socio-demographic characteristics
►No dissection of selection and influence regarding the role of status 
characteristics for network structure
►Individual outcomes not considered, how does the economy of attaining 
positions in the field change over time?
►Measurement does not allow for a test of different mechanisms that are 
related to increasing inequality (hoarding resources vs. cognitive explanation)
►Content of research not considered 
►Investigation of one field, how does it generalize to other fields?

Additional Slides


--- PAGE BREAK ---

►2019 Stochastic-Actor Oriented models (SAOMs; Snijders et al. 2011); 
abandoned because it’s currently not legitimate to compare estimates 
between models of different networks (especially if the size changes); Tom 
Snijders is working on it though….
►2020 Network regressions as simpler method; problem: similar issues in 
comparing coefficients (see logistic regressions Mood 2010)  
►2021 Simulations (cf. Bearman et al. 2004): simulating networks and study 
their global properties; cannot account for tie-formation mechanisms 
simultaneously, no solid statistical inference; advantage: you can get rid of the 
mechanical link between size and inequality (Anderson et al. 1999)
►2022 Exponential random graph models (ERGMs; Lusher et al. 2013; Duxburry
2021); drawbacks: no separate parameters for selection influence, poor GOF, 
advantages: allow for comparison of effect size over time
